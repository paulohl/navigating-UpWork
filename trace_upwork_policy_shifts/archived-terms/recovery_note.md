Recovered 29 October 2026. This note supersedes the provisional June 2020 evidence assessment in the original chronology.

## What changed

**Before: version 4.5, section 7.1.** The agreement already prohibited off-platform payment circumvention for 24 months, subject to conversion provisions and the pre-existing-relationship carve-out. It also said:

> You further agree you will not initiate unsolicited communications with any User outside of the Site

This quotation is an excerpt from a longer paragraph. Thus June 2020 did not introduce every restriction on external contact from scratch.

**After: version 5.0, new section 7.2, PDF pp.18–19.** The agreement added “COMMUNICATING THROUGH THE SITE; NOT SHARING CONTACT DETAILS.” The former section 7.2, “Opting Out,” became section 7.3. The new communication provision includes:

> The provisions of this Section 7.2 apply to any interaction between Users where the Client has a Basic or Plus Account.

> The provisions of this Section 7.2 do not apply to any interaction between Users where the Client is an Enterprise Client.

The next sentence defines Enterprise Client to include a legacy Enterprise client or an Upwork Business Client **with the Enterprise badge on its job post or search tile**. This is more precise than treating every business customer as exempt.

Before a Service Contract, covered users agree that they:

> will use Upwork as the sole manner to communicate with other Users

The clause also prohibits providing, seeking or using “Means of Direct Contact,” including contact information discoverable through links, profiles, proposals, job posts, invitations, pre-hire communications and attached files, except as otherwise provided on the Site. Its definition includes phone numbers, email, physical addresses, contact forms, external application systems and communications-enabled social/platform accounts.

The sanction is explicit:

> Your Account may be permanently suspended if you violate this Section 7.2.

“May” authorizes a sanction; it does not establish that suspension occurred automatically or equally across users. The exception applies to the communication section; it is not blanket permission to move payments off-platform.

## Other terms recovered from the same captures

- **Fee and ACH Authorization Agreement, effective 23 October 2019** — original PDF `5db0b5adc5f5ce2545408024`; section 2.1 and its worked examples document tiered relationship-based pricing. Different pricing provisions apply to specified exceptions. This proves the schedule existed in that agreement, not its first introduction in 2016.
- **Freelancer Membership Agreement, effective 23 October 2019** — original PDF `5db0b65a54e1dc2f0f7a875d`; section 2 makes Connects purchasable at the advertised price and reserves changes to fees, token allowances and token prices on reasonable advance notice. It does **not** itself fix the price at $0.15 or prove the initial 2019 rollout date.

The bundle also includes three additional version-hosted User Agreement PDFs found during discovery (`606b71…`, `61b0b4…`, `64a63e…`). Their effective dates were not independently established in this recovery; they are supplemental, not the basis for the dated April–June comparison. Their opaque identifiers must not be treated as effective dates.

## Preservation and limits

The ZIP contains the two full captured Legal Center HTML responses, two extracted User Agreement HTML files, seven original-host PDF downloads and a JSON manifest with SHA-256 hashes. The extracted HTML files are clearly labelled transformations, not original standalone downloads. Internet Archive replay links within the captured pages may depend on network access; the extracted agreement text and downloaded PDFs can be read locally.

The full Legal Center pages contain multiple contracts and transitional navigation; the comparison uses the primary User Agreement block, contract ID 15858, rather than assuming every agreement in a page was operative. Dates for the two 2019 subsidiary agreements come from their own effective-date labels in the captured page. PDFs were downloaded from the original version host in 2026, not represented as historical Wayback PDF captures. Hashes identify the recovered bytes, not legal authenticity or a verified chain of custody from 2020.

This recovery establishes the wording and dated rule change. It does not determine whether a particular suspension was justified, whether a particular account received notice, or how the rules were enforced in practice. No complete 2023 fee-agreement revision history or 2016 launch terms have been recovered in this focused pass.
